A plane worth saving

In 1997, MicroProse released Magic: The Gathering for the PC and hid something remarkable inside it: Shandalar, a whole adventure built around the card game. You roam an overworld of towns, dungeons and castles, buy and trade cards, take on quests, and win every fight with a Magic duel, where the loser forfeits the ante.

Five Guild Lords race to cast the Spell of Dominion, not knowing that it is a trap set by Arzakon. Only you, a wandering novice spellcaster, know the truth.

The adventure

Shandalar is a Magic: The Gathering role-playing game. This is the adventure Shandalar Forever is rebuilding.

Roam a living world

Cross plains, forests, swamps, islands and mountains. Each land has its own creatures, and they come looking for you.

Duel for ante

Every fight is a game of Magic. Win and take your opponent's ante card; lose and it takes yours.

Build your collection

Buy and sell cards in villages, and trade mana stones with the minor wizards in the cities for rare spells.

Quests and mana links

Take on quests for the villages. Each success can link you to a city's magic, and every link adds to your life.

Dungeons and castles

Make beaten creatures talk. Only they know where the hidden dungeons are, and what waits in the Lords' castles.

World Magics

Leap across the land, double your speed, or blast a creature from your path, if you have the mana stones to pay.

What is Shandalar Forever?

Faithful to 1997

The original artwork, layouts and interaction style are the reference. Each feature is checked against the recovered 1.0 game, with traces of its behaviour and side-by-side screenshots.

Native, not emulated

No Wine and no Windows modules. The game is written in Rust with the Fyrox engine, and the same build also runs in the browser through WebAssembly.

Built to grow

A module system is on the way, so the game can be expanded with new cards and mechanics while the original 1997 adventure stays intact.
